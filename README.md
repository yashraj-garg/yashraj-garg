# Yashraj Garg

Undergraduate at Trinity College, University of Cambridge, working across
statistical inference, computational neuroscience, and data-intensive research.

I am particularly interested in how quantitative models and computational
methods can be used to study individual differences in human behaviour,
especially when scientific conclusions depend on measurement, model
specification, and the choice of statistical estimand.

## Current research

### Emotional memory and multimodal neuroimaging · University of Basel

I am leading the quantitative analyses and manuscript writing for a first-author
study of individual differences in emotional-memory enhancement.

The project integrates large-scale behavioural and multimodal neuroimaging data
and has involved:

- categorical and population-continuous statistical estimands;
- mixed-effects and linear modelling;
- corrected high-dimensional neuroimaging inference;
- whole-brain and region-of-interest analyses;
- fixed-component ICA;
- structural morphometry;
- multiplicity control, influence diagnostics, and sensitivity analyses;
- reproducible R and HPC-based analysis workflows.

**Status:** First-author manuscript in preparation; I lead the quantitative analyses and manuscript writing.

## Selected quantitative work

### Statistical inference in R

Applied statistical modelling, model diagnostics, transformations,
parametric and non-parametric inference, effect-size estimation, and
assumption checking in assessed Cambridge coursework and empirical research.

### Mathematical modelling and dynamical systems

Implemented and analysed ordinary-differential-equation models in R,
including numerical SIR simulation, equilibrium and stability analysis,
Jacobians and eigenvalues, nullclines, phase-plane dynamics, and numerical
final-size solutions.

### Computational biology

Worked with biological sequence-analysis workflows including protein homology
search, multiple-sequence alignment, and phylogenetic inference.
